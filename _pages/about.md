## Research Position

My research is grounded in the belief that design can make technological futures discussable before
they become settled infrastructure. I work with emerging systems such as <strong>large language
models</strong>, <strong>smart homes</strong>, <strong>autonomous vehicles</strong>, and <strong>AI-enabled
safeguarding</strong>, studying not only whether these technologies work, but how they redistribute
agency, care, responsibility, and power.

Rather than treating users as sources of requirements, I use participatory and speculative methods to
invite people into design conversations. These methods help surface tensions that are often invisible
in technology-centered development: who gets to configure a system, whose interpretation counts as
evidence, what kinds of everyday expertise are overlooked, and how design can resist purely
solutionist narratives.
